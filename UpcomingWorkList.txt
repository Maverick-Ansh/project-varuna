------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION A - WHAT IS STILL UNSOLVED (the honest gaps)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

G1.  Co-location on flats is unsolved. The twin predicts flooded AREA but not
     WHICH street (CSI ~ 0.033). We proved WHY (DEM noise > relief) but have
     not BEATEN it.
G2.  No storm-sewer network or pumping is modelled. Predictions over-estimate
     flooding near working drains - the biggest physics gap.
G3.  60 m hydraulics are coarser than the street graph. Carved "drains" are
     full 60 m cells, so absolute excavation/volume numbers are artifacts.
G4.  Groundwater/recharge still runs on SAMPLE data for most areas - rankings
     mean nothing until real CGWB / India-WRIS data goes in (only V3
     Karnataka has metered plans).
G5.  No aquifer response model. We route water TOWARD recharge but never
     simulate the water-table RISE - the flood-to-drought loop is open.
G6.  Mumbai physics incomplete: closed-boundary tiles, no cross-seam flow, no
     tidal / storm-surge coupling (usually THE binding constraint for Mumbai
     outfalls). SAR + DEM-ensemble validation never run for Mumbai.
G7.  FloodGNN only distills the twin -> inherits its biases; never trained on
     real observed street water.
G8.  The nightly citizen-learning loop has never actually run on real reports
     (deployed but unexercised).
G9.  Intervention DELTAS are never validated against before/after SAR where
     cities actually built drainage - benefit is only simulated.
G10. Costs are a single indicative RCC rate - no land, utilities, O&M, or
     damage-avoided / ROI accounting.
G11. No real-time nowcasting - everything is design-storm / hindcast.
G12. The paper is done but unsubmitted - no venue, no benchmark release.

SECTION B - 30 IDEAS TO CHOOSE FROM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--- Close the honest gaps (turn admissions into contributions) ---

1.(*) Robust / uncertainty-aware planning. Optimise interventions to be robust
      across the DEM ensemble (CVaR / min-regret). Turns the negative result
      into a method: "can't co-locate, so plan for the distribution."
      Effort: Med | G1

2.(*) Probabilistic per-street flood maps. Ship flood PROBABILITY per street
      (from the ensemble) instead of one deterministic map. Honest and far
      more decision-useful.
      Effort: Med | G1

3.    1D sewer + 2D surface dual drainage. Couple a lightweight SWMM-style 1D
      pipe network to the 2D twin - the missing storm-sewer layer.
      Effort: High | G2

4.(*) Sub-grid street conveyance. Treat streets as 1D channels WITHIN 60 m
      cells so carved drains stop being full-cell artifacts.
      Effort: High | G3

5.    CartoDEM / LiDAR co-location experiment. Rebuild one ward on 10 m
      CartoDEM (or drone photogrammetry) and test whether finer relief
      RESOLVES co-location. A clean, publishable test of our own thesis.
      Effort: Med | G1

6.(*) SAR-supervised FloodGNN. Train the GNN directly on observed Sentinel-1
      street water, removing the "distills the twin" caveat. Potentially the
      biggest single scientific upgrade.
      Effort: Med | G7

7.    Validate intervention deltas vs before/after SAR. Find a city that
      actually built drainage; score the CHANGE, not just state.
      Effort: Med | G9

8.    Tidal / storm-surge boundary for coastal tiles. Add a tide/surge
      boundary (global tide model + gauge) for Mumbai outfalls.
      Effort: High | G6

9.    Run Mumbai SAR + DEM-ensemble validation. Finish the validation we
      already have machinery for, on the tiles we skipped.
      Effort: Low | G6

--- The recharge / flood-to-drought thesis (our most distinctive claim) ---

10.(*) Real groundwater data + aquifer response model. Ingest India-WRIS/CGWB,
       then couple a MODFLOW-lite / analytical model to simulate water-table
       RISE from recharge - closes the flood->drought loop quantitatively.
       Effort: High | G4/G5

11.    Flood-cut <-> recharge Pareto optimiser. Multi-objective siting:
       maximise flood reduction AND aquifer recharge jointly.
       Effort: Med | G4

12.    Arsenic / water-quality risk layer. A safety gate on recharge siting
       (Bihar shallow alluvium) - flagged as needed but not modelled.
       Effort: Low | G4

13.    Metered recharge accounting nationwide. Extend V3's Karnataka metered
       recharge plans to all areas with a real water-balance ledger.
       Effort: Med | G4

--- ML / GNN frontiers ---

14.(*) End-to-end differentiable portfolio design. Use the DIFFERENTIABLE twin:
       gradient-optimise drainage + storage jointly under a budget for a
       target cut. A true end-to-end design few prior works can do.
       Effort: Med | G3

15.    FloodGNN -> cross-city foundation model. Pretrain on many cities,
       few-shot a new one. Scale the zero-shot transfer result.
       Effort: High

16.    Physics-informed (mass-conserving) emulator loss. Add conservation
       constraints to the U-Net / GNN to see if co-location improves.
       Effort: Med | G1

17.    Optimal IoT sensor placement. Active-learning / D-optimal design: where
       to put N cheap water-level sensors to shrink twin uncertainty most.
       Effort: Med | G8

18.    Generative super-resolution of depth. Diffusion/CNN upsampling of 60 m
       depth to street scale conditioned on high-res features. Risky,
       out-of-box, potentially striking.
       Effort: High | G3

--- Deployment, participation & real-world use ---

19.(*) Real-time nowcasting. Ingest live IMD radar / GPM IMERG / Open-Meteo
       nowcast -> run the emulator live for "next 3 hours." Turns a research
       demo into an operational alert system.
       Effort: Med | G11

20.    Actually run the nightly citizen loop. Seed real reports (even a small
       pilot), let the reward gate fire once, report the first accepted /
       refused update - the most interesting experiment not yet run.
       Effort: Low | G8

21.    CV flood-depth from citizen photos. Estimate depth from uploaded street
       photos (reference objects: curbs, tyres) - richer than depth bands.
       Effort: Med | G8

22.    News / social auto-event detection. Finish the "news stage 2.5" hook:
       scrape flood mentions to auto-trigger validation and harvest ground
       truth.
       Effort: Med | G8/G9

23.    Drainage-maintenance prioritiser. Simulate "this drain is 50% clogged"
       -> which drains, if cleaned, cut the most flooding. Cheap, actionable,
       exactly what a municipality can do tomorrow.
       Effort: Low | G2

24.    Offline PWA: report -> instant reroute. Mobile app with offline tiles:
       drop a report, get a flood-safe route immediately.
       Effort: Med

--- Genuinely out-of-the-box / moonshots ---

25.(*) Damage-avoided ROI engine. Depth-damage curves x OSM building exposure
       -> rupees of damage avoided vs cost. Rank interventions by RETURN, not
       m3. Speaks the language decision-makers actually use.
       Effort: Med | G10

26.    Equity-weighted planning. Overlay slum / census / vulnerability data ->
       prioritise by WHO is most exposed, not just water volume. A justice
       angle almost no flood model has.
       Effort: Med

27.    Flood + heat co-benefit optimiser. Green-blue infrastructure that cuts
       BOTH flooding and urban heat (WorldCover / LST) - multi-hazard design.
       Effort: High

28.    Climate-2050 stress test. Re-run interventions under CMIP-downscaled
       future design storms - do they still hold?
       Effort: Med

29.    Development-permit screening ("what if we pave this?"). Simulate a
       WorldCover change (green patch -> concrete) and show the flood delta.
       A regulatory-grade what-if tool.
       Effort: Low

30.    Agent-based evacuation + shelter siting. Put people/traffic on the
       flooded street graph (SUMO / mesa), find where they get stuck,
       optimise shelter locations.
       Effort: High

--- Bonus (community / impact, not R&D) ---

B.     Release the flat-city honest-negative as a public benchmark + finally
       submit the paper. Low effort, high credibility. | G12
